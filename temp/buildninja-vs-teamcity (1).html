Real user feedback

Why teams leave TeamCity in 2026

These issues come from published reviews on Capterra, Gartner Peer Insights, and G2 - consistent patterns across independent reviewers, not edge cases.

💸

The agent cost cliff

The Professional free tier locks teams at 3 agents and 100 configurations. Once outgrown, you face a $2,351/yr Enterprise license plus $359/yr per additional agent - $5,541/yr for a 10-agent setup in licensing alone.

🖥️

Heavy JVM resource footprint

TeamCity runs on the JVM and demands significant RAM to keep the UI responsive. Gartner reviewers flag the server requirement as disproportionately high, noting that without enough memory the interface becomes sluggish and build queues slow.

⚙️

Configuration complexity at scale

Multiple long-term TeamCity users - including those with a decade on the platform - report that the admin panel is difficult to navigate and reconfiguring existing projects after initial setup is consistently error-prone.

📋

Renewal pricing restructuring

JetBrains changed on-premises renewal pricing in late 2025, removing the 50% renewal discount for customers who miss the expiry window by more than one month - an unexpected cost pressure for teams that don't track license dates precisely.

✅ How BuildNinja resolves each of these

  • →No agent fees - unlimited build agents are included in the Growth Edition at zero cost. No upgrade required, no ceiling to hit, ever.
  • →No JVM dependency - BuildNinja runs with a lightweight footprint on a 4-core, 8 GB VPS with room to spare for multiple concurrent agents.
  • →UI-first configuration - most pipelines are set up through the dashboard without touching YAML. The Dojo sandbox lets you prototype before touching production.
  • →No renewal risk - the Growth Edition has no annual license, no renewal window, and no discount tiers to manage. Your CI/CD cost stays $0 permanently.

Migration guide

Moving from TeamCity to BuildNinja

Most TeamCity-to-BuildNinja migrations complete in a few hours to a couple of days, depending on pipeline count and complexity.

✅ What transfers directly

→Build logic - compile, test, and deploy steps map directly to BuildNinja step types
→Environment variables and secrets - re-enter in BuildNinja's Build Parameters
→VCS integrations - GitHub, GitLab, and Bitbucket connect via OAuth in minutes
→Agent machines - existing Windows/Linux/macOS machines become BuildNinja agents

⚠ What requires rebuilding

→Kotlin DSL configuration - BuildNinja uses its own format (most pipelines rebuild in under an hour)
→Complex DAG build chains - verify against v1.2.0 for advanced cases
→Custom TeamCity plugins with no direct equivalent - evaluate case-by-case

Recommended migration steps

1

Deploy BuildNinja alongside TeamCity. Both run simultaneously during transition - no production disruption on day one.

2

Use the Dojo sandbox to prototype your first two or three pipelines before touching any production infrastructure.

3

Migrate low-risk projects first - feature branch CI, documentation builds, and internal tooling pipelines.

4

Move production deployment pipelines last, after validating behaviour on lower-risk builds over one or two sprint cycles.

5

Decommission TeamCity once all pipelines are validated and the entire team is comfortable with BuildNinja's workflow.
